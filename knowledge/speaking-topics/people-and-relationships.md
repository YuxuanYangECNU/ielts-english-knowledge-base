# People & Relationships — Family Relationships

> **Quick answer**  
> Focus on relationship words that are easy to confuse but useful in IELTS Speaking. Each term uses a **Chinese quick gloss** for fast recognition and a **standard English definition/context** for precise use.

## 1 · Core relationship words

| Word / phrase | 中文速览 | English definition & context | IELTS use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **cousin** | 堂/表兄弟姐妹 | the child of your aunt or uncle; English does not normally distinguish male/female or paternal/maternal cousins | Useful when describing extended family |
| **nephew** | 侄子 / 外甥 | the son of your brother or sister | Common family-tree relationship |
| **niece** | 侄女 / 外甥女 | the daughter of your brother or sister | Pair with **nephew** |
| **uncle** | 叔/伯/舅/姨父/姑父等 | the brother of your mother or father, or the husband of your aunt | English uses one broad term where Chinese has several |
| **aunt** | 姑/姨/婶/舅妈等 | the sister of your mother or father, or the wife of your uncle | Broad family term; pair with **uncle** |
| **sibling** | 兄弟姐妹 | a brother or sister; a gender-neutral collective term | Very useful in Speaking: *I have two siblings.* |
| **relative** | 亲戚 | a person connected to you by family relationship | Broad general term: *close relatives, distant relatives* |
| **spouse** | 配偶 | a husband or wife; neutral but slightly formal | More common in formal contexts than casual speaking |
| **partner** | 伴侣 | the person you are in a romantic relationship with; often more natural and inclusive than **spouse** in everyday English | Useful when marital status is not important |

## 2 · The in-law system

**in-law** means a family relationship created through marriage rather than blood.

| Word / phrase | 中文速览 | English definition & context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **father-in-law** | 岳父 / 公公 | your husband’s or wife’s father |
| **mother-in-law** | 岳母 / 婆婆 | your husband’s or wife’s mother |
| **son-in-law** | 女婿 | your daughter’s husband |
| **daughter-in-law** | 儿媳 | your son’s wife |
| **brother-in-law** | 姐/妹夫；妻/夫的兄弟等 | commonly your spouse’s brother or your sibling’s husband |
| **sister-in-law** | 嫂/弟媳；妻/夫的姐妹等 | commonly your spouse’s sister or your sibling’s wife |

### Direction matters

Using the family tree in the study material:

- **Yuri and Aiko are cousins.** Their parents are siblings.
- **Yuri is Akira’s nephew.** Yuri is the son of Akira’s sibling.
- **Akira is Yuri’s uncle.** This is the same relationship viewed in the opposite direction.
- **Sora is Ren’s son-in-law** if Sora is married to Ren’s daughter.
- **Ren is Sora’s father-in-law.** Again, the same marriage relationship viewed from the opposite direction.
- **Mio is Ren’s daughter-in-law** if Mio is married to Ren’s son.

This “direction” is the main reason **in-law** vocabulary feels confusing.

## 3 · Family-group terms worth knowing

| Word / phrase | 中文速览 | English definition & context | Natural example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **immediate family** | 直系/最亲近家庭成员 | your closest family members, typically parents, siblings, spouse and children; exact definitions can vary by context | *Most of my immediate family live in the same city.* |
| **extended family** | 大家庭 / 旁系亲属 | relatives beyond the immediate family, such as grandparents, aunts, uncles and cousins | *I grew up in a large extended family.* |
| **only child** | 独生子女 | a person who has no brothers or sisters | *I’m an only child, so I spent a lot of time with my cousins.* |
| **twin sister / twin brother** | 双胞胎姐妹 / 兄弟 | a sister or brother born at the same time as you | *She’s my twin sister, but we have very different personalities.* |

## 4 · Speaking expressions that matter more than rare kinship words

Knowing the noun is useful; describing the relationship is more important for IELTS Speaking.

- **be close to someone** — 和某人关系亲近  
  *I’m particularly close to one of my cousins.*
- **get along well with someone** — 和某人相处融洽  
  *I get along really well with my older sister.*
- **have a close-knit family** — 家庭关系紧密  
  *We’re quite a close-knit family and usually spend holidays together.*
- **look up to someone** — 敬佩 / 尊敬某人  
  *I’ve always looked up to my uncle because he’s very patient and reliable.*

## High-value contrasts

### cousin vs nephew / niece

- **cousin** → your aunt’s or uncle’s child.
- **nephew / niece** → your brother’s or sister’s child.

### relative vs sibling

- **relative** is broad: any family relation.
- **sibling** means specifically a brother or sister.

### spouse vs partner

- **spouse** specifically means husband or wife and sounds somewhat formal.
- **partner** is broader and often more natural in everyday conversation.

## Active recall

Without looking back:

1. If your sister has a son, what is he to you?
2. What is the difference between **cousin** and **nephew**?
3. If someone is your daughter’s husband, what is he to you?
4. Explain the difference between **immediate family** and **extended family**.
5. Describe one family member using **be close to**, **get along with**, or **look up to**.
